Venezuela's oil sector has been through one of the most dramatic cycles in the history of the global energy industry — from a peak of 3.5 million barrels per day in the late 1990s to a trough below 700,000 bpd, and now showing early signs of stabilization and modest recovery. Understanding where production stands today, and what drives the path forward, is essential context for any investment decision.

## Where Production Stands Today

Venezuela's oil output has stabilized in the range of 800,000–900,000 bpd as of early 2026, according to OPEC secondary source estimates. This represents a meaningful improvement from the lows of 2020–2021, driven primarily by:

- Continued operations by Chevron under its OFAC license, contributing an estimated 150,000–200,000 bpd
- Incremental recovery in several Orinoco Belt blocks through targeted maintenance and workovers
- Modest improvements in PDVSA's internal operational capacity

This is still far below Venezuela's potential. Even a return to 2 million bpd — which represents less than 60% of the country's historic peak — would require sustained capital investment and operational improvements across dozens of fields.

## The Key Production Drivers

**Chevron's continued role**
Chevron's maintained presence in Venezuela — operating through its JV interests in Petropiar and Petroboscan — provides an important operational anchor. Chevron's ability to continue operating under U.S. Treasury licensing demonstrates that the regulatory environment, while complex, is navigable for well-structured partnerships.

**ESP restoration programs**
A significant number of Orinoco Belt wells are currently offline due to failed electrical submersible pumps. Systematic ESP replacement and maintenance programs have proven to be one of the highest-return production restoration activities available, with wells returning to production typically within weeks of intervention.

**Diluent availability**
The ability to source and deliver diluents — required to move extra-heavy crude through Venezuela's pipeline system — has been a persistent constraint. Improved supply chain management is gradually easing this bottleneck in active operating areas.

**Gas infrastructure**
Associated gas management remains a challenge. Flaring is widespread due to inadequate gas gathering infrastructure, representing both an environmental issue and a lost revenue opportunity. Gas reinjection programs, where infrastructure permits, support reservoir pressure maintenance and improve oil recovery rates.

## The Geopolitical Dimension

Venezuela's oil sector does not operate in a geopolitical vacuum. U.S. sanctions policy, the relationship between Caracas and Washington, and Venezuela's relationships with China and Russia all affect the operating environment for international investors.

Key observations for 2026:

- The U.S. has maintained specific licensing arrangements for certain operators, creating a differentiated environment where well-structured partnerships can operate
- China remains Venezuela's largest creditor and has strong incentives to see Venezuelan production recover to service its loan obligations
- The Venezuelan government has demonstrated consistent willingness to engage with international operators who bring genuine operational capability

None of this makes Venezuela a simple or low-risk environment. But the framing of Venezuela as categorically uninvestable misses the nuance of a sector where experienced operators with the right legal structure have continued to operate successfully through multiple cycles.

## What Would Accelerate Recovery

The clearest path to meaningfully higher Venezuelan production runs through three things:

1. **Sustained capital investment** in well workovers, artificial lift restoration, and facility maintenance — this is the highest-return near-term lever
2. **Experienced oil field services capacity** — capital without operational expertise does not produce oil; the two must come together
3. **Continued regulatory engagement** — maintaining the licensing frameworks that allow international operators to function

These are precisely the inputs that well-structured E&P partnerships are designed to deliver. The production potential is not in question. The question is whether the right combination of capital, expertise, and legal structure can be assembled at sufficient scale — and that is what serious Venezuelan oil investors are focused on.
