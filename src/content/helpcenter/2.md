## Is it legal to invest in Venezuela?

Yes, subject to applicable sanctions regulations in your jurisdiction.

U.S. persons and entities need to be aware of OFAC sanctions, which restrict certain transactions involving Venezuela and PDVSA. However, OFAC issues specific licenses that authorize defined categories of activity — including oil and gas operations for certain qualified operators. Non-U.S. investors face fewer restrictions.

DerivaConnect's legal team ensures all investment structures comply with applicable regulations in relevant jurisdictions. We will not onboard investors whose participation would create a compliance issue.

## Hasn't Venezuela nationalized foreign oil assets before?

Yes — most notably in 2007, when President Chávez renegotiated the terms under which international majors operated in the Orinoco Belt. Companies that accepted the new joint venture terms (including Chevron, Total, ENI, and Statoil) continued operating without interruption. Companies that refused (ExxonMobil, ConocoPhillips) had their assets expropriated and received compensation through international arbitration.

The lesson from this history is not that Venezuela is uninvestable — it is that the structure of the investment matters enormously. Joint ventures with clear contractual terms and international arbitration clauses have fared far better than assets held under informal arrangements.

## What is Venezuela's current oil production level?

Venezuelan production has stabilized in the range of 800,000–900,000 barrels per day as of early 2026, according to OPEC secondary source data. This is significantly below Venezuela's historic peak of approximately 3.5 million bpd but represents a meaningful recovery from the lows of 2020–2021.

The gap between current and potential production is the core of the investment thesis: Venezuela's reserves can sustain production levels many times higher than current output.

## What is PDVSA?

Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. (PDVSA) is Venezuela's state-owned oil company. Under Venezuela's constitution, all hydrocarbon resources belong to the state, and PDVSA is the primary vehicle through which the state manages oil and gas production.

International operators and investors engage with Venezuela's oil sector through formal agreements with PDVSA or the Venezuelan Ministry of Petroleum — typically structured as joint ventures in which PDVSA holds a majority stake.

## What is the Orinoco Oil Belt?

The Orinoco Oil Belt is a 55,000 square kilometer formation in central Venezuela containing the world's largest accumulation of extra-heavy crude oil. It holds the majority of Venezuela's 303 billion barrels of proven reserves and is the primary production area for new E&P activity in the country.

Extra-heavy crude requires processing (either upgrading or blending with diluents) before it can be transported and refined, but well-developed technology exists for both approaches.

Production-linked revenue structures are directly affected by oil price — higher prices mean higher revenue per barrel produced. Fixed-fee oil field services structures are less exposed to oil price movements.
